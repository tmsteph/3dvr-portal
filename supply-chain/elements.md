# Open Elements Commons

## Vision

Trace the physical world all the way down to useful elements and feedstocks, then build community capability to recover, gather, refine, reuse, and eventually produce more of them responsibly.

This is the materials layer underneath Open Electronics, Open Linen, Open Food, construction, energy, tools, and future community manufacturing.

The goal is not isolated self-sufficiency or reckless backyard mining. The goal is practical material literacy and progressively more local, open, circular production.

## Order of operations

1. **Use less** — design products that need fewer scarce or hazardous materials.
2. **Repair** — keep existing products functioning.
3. **Salvage** — recover usable parts before destroying them for raw material.
4. **Urban mine** — recover metals, glass, polymers, magnets, batteries, wire, boards, motors, and other feedstocks from discarded products and infrastructure.
5. **Recycle and refine** — turn clean, well-characterized scrap into useful feedstock.
6. **Gather renewable or abundant materials** — biomass, clay, sand, stone, natural fibres, carbon sources, and other locally appropriate materials.
7. **Extract virgin resources only where justified** — quarrying, mining, brines, ores, and other primary sources require legal access, environmental review, safety engineering, and restoration plans.

## First practical material loops

Start with loops that are understandable, valuable, and achievable without pretending to be a mine or refinery:

### Copper loop
Discarded wire / motors / transformers → identify and sort → reuse intact wire where possible → recover clean copper scrap → documented recycler or safe industrial remelt partner → new wire, bus bars, sheet, or feedstock.

### Aluminum loop
Discarded extrusion / enclosure / heat sink / can stock → sort alloys when possible → direct reuse first → clean recycling stream → casting/extrusion/feedstock partner → new mechanical parts.

### Steel loop
Frames / fasteners / appliances / machines → salvage usable parts → sort → local fabrication and welding → scrap stream → regional steel recycling.

### Electronics loop
Whole device → repair → harvest modules → harvest components → separate boards / wire / batteries / displays / metal → route each stream to an appropriate recovery process.

## From recycling to primary materials

Only after we understand secondary material loops should we systematically study primary extraction.

For each candidate local or regional source, ask:

1. What useful material is present and at what concentration?
2. Who owns or governs the land/material?
3. Is collecting or extracting it legal?
4. What ecological system would be disturbed?
5. What water, energy, chemicals, equipment, and labor are required?
6. What waste, tailings, emissions, or contamination could result?
7. Can the land or material source be restored afterward?
8. Is recovery from waste actually cheaper or cleaner?
9. Does the community genuinely need this material locally?

## Community infrastructure ladder

### Stage 1 — Material library
Physical samples, datasheets, scrap identification guides, teardown data, and simple testing.

### Stage 2 — Repair and salvage shop
Hand tools, soldering/rework, mechanical repair, wire processing, fastener storage, component bins, and safe battery handling.

### Stage 3 — Sorting and preparation
Scales, labeling, alloy/material identification, stripping, cleaning, crushing or size reduction only where safe, and separated outbound recycling streams.

### Stage 4 — Fabrication from recovered stock
Machining, welding, casting where appropriate, sheet work, wire forming, ceramics, glass reuse, plastics processing, and composite experimentation.

### Stage 5 — Community materials lab
Characterization, microscopy, electrical/thermal testing, controlled furnaces, vacuum equipment, chemistry only with appropriate facilities, ventilation, waste handling, and trained operators.

### Stage 6 — Responsible regional extraction pilots
Partner with universities, existing mines/quarries/recyclers, tribes/indigenous communities, landowners, municipalities, cooperatives, and environmental experts before considering any primary extraction project.

## Inspirations

- **Open Source Ecology:** community-scale industrial machinery, modular fabrication, closed-loop manufacturing, and open production systems.
- **Hacker Fab:** open, reproducible process equipment and process documentation for semiconductor fabrication.
- **Balthazar:** a concrete open-computing product that gives the material stack something real to build toward.

## Principles

- Salvage before extraction.
- Circular before linear.
- Measure before claiming.
- Know the material and its contamination before processing it.
- Do not externalize toxic waste onto workers, neighbors, water, or land.
- Respect land ownership, local communities, indigenous rights, environmental law, and permitting.
- Publish failures and hazards as carefully as successes.
- Prefer processes communities can inspect, repair, understand, and safely teach.
- Treat restoration and end-of-life as part of the design.

## North star

A future 3DVR community should be able to point at a computer, tool, garment, building, or energy system and answer:

**What is it made of? Where did those materials come from? Who processed them? Can we recover them? What could we make locally next time?**

Eventually, for some materials, the answer may begin with material gathered or extracted by the community itself. But the deepest goal is not mining. It is stewardship of matter.
