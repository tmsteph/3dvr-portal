Make Your Own Sandals

Two simple ways to make footwear with your hands: durable minimalist huaraches and traditional Japanese-inspired rope waraji. Start small, test carefully, repair often.

Huaraches: easiestWaraji: wovenFit & safety

01 / Huaraches

Beginner · about 1–2 hours

Best first pair for walking. A flexible sole and one continuous lace per foot.

Materials

  • Flexible 4–6 mm rubber sole sheet, enough for both feet (avoid slick rubber)
  • About 2–3 m of soft, strong 3–4 mm cord, cut into two lengths
  • Paper, pencil, scissors or utility knife, ruler and leather punch/awl
  • Optional: sandpaper to smooth the edges

Build

  1. Stand on paper and trace each foot while bearing weight. Mark the outline 5–8 mm beyond your foot; leave room for toe spread.
  2. Cut paper templates and check that neither heel nor toes hang off. Transfer to rubber and cut carefully on a protected surface.
  3. Mark a toe hole between the big and second toe, slightly forward of the webbing. Mark two side holes near the ankle, positioned so the lace will not rub the ankle bones. Test placement on the paper first.
  4. Punch clean holes sized for the cord. Round and smooth sharp edges.
  5. Thread the lace through the toe hole and secure it underneath with a low-profile stopper knot or suitable anchor. Route both sides toward the ankle holes and lace around the heel/ankle so the sandal stays put without constricting.
  6. Try on indoors. Adjust until the sole follows your foot without sliding. Trim and melt synthetic cord ends only with appropriate ventilation and fire precautions, or whip natural cord ends with thread.
First test: Walk 5–10 minutes on clean, level ground. Check for hot spots, lace pressure and slipping before longer walks.

02 / Waraji

Beginner weaving · about 2–4 hours

Traditional Japanese waraji were made in many regional styles, often from rice straw. For a first experiment, soft cotton rope is easier on the hands. This is a simplified practice interpretation, not a historical reconstruction.

Materials

  • About 15–25 m of 4–6 mm soft cotton rope for a trial pair (actual usage varies with size and weave)
  • Four sturdy anchor points or a simple weaving jig, plus scissors and measuring tape
  • Optional: scrap cord for temporary ties

Build

  1. Measure your foot length and width. Set up two parallel pairs of tensioned longitudinal cords on a secure jig, slightly wider than the foot.
  2. Leave long ends for tying. Weave a separate working cord over and under the longitudinal cords, alternating direction each row.
  3. Push each row snugly against the previous row. Shape the toe and heel gradually, checking width against your foot template.
  4. At the front, form secure toe loops or a toe-strap attachment from the structural cords; keep knots away from pressure points.
  5. Finish the heel with tightly secured weaving and leave long, paired ties. Test the weave by pulling firmly by hand before wearing.
  6. Fit the ties around the heel and ankle, keeping them snug but not tight. Trim only after a successful indoor fitting.
Important: Rope construction varies by tradition. This beginner weave is a prototype; inspect the attachments before every use. Cotton wears and becomes slippery when wet.

Fit, safety & repair

  • Minimalist footwear changes loading on feet and calves. Start with short sessions and increase gradually; stop if pain develops.
  • Neither style provides puncture protection or the slip resistance of certified work shoes. Do not use them for stagehand work, construction, or hazardous surfaces.
  • Do not hike steep, wet or rocky terrain in an untested pair.
  • Replace frayed cords, loose knots and worn soles promptly. Save templates and record improvements for the next pair.
  • For public paths, consider a textured rubber sole. Pure rope waraji are better as a craft experiment until thoroughly tested.
