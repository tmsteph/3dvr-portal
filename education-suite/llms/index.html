How large language models work

A Transformer is a machine for routing and transforming information. An LLM is a very large Transformer trained to predict what comes next. Scale that simple objective far enough and it learns surprisingly useful representations of language, code, concepts, and procedures.

tokens→embeddings→attention→MLPs→layers→next token

01 · Transformer

The architecture underneath modern LLMs

Transformers process tokens through repeated blocks of attention and feed-forward neural networks. Instead of reading only one word at a time, attention lets each token gather useful information from other tokens in its context.

Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dk)V
Q · Query

What information am I looking for?

K · Key

What kind of information do I contain?

V · Value

What information can I contribute?

02 · Self-attention

Watch context change meaning

Tap a token. The brighter connections represent a simplified illustration of which earlier words could matter to that token. Real models learn these relationships numerically across many attention heads.

Example: “The robot picked up the battery because it was empty.” What does it refer to?

03 · Many heads, many layers

Communication + computation

Attention

moves information between token representations.

MLP

transforms information within each position.

The residual stream acts like a shared workspace flowing through the network. Every block reads from it and writes useful changes back into it. Stack many blocks and simple representations can become increasingly contextual and abstract.

04 · Training

A remarkably simple objective

“The capital of France is ___” → predict Paris.

Training repeats this prediction problem across enormous datasets. Errors are measured and the network's parameters are adjusted. To become very good at prediction, the model benefits from learning structure: grammar, relationships, programming patterns, facts, styles, and procedures.

The objective is “predict the next token.” A useful internal strategy can become something closer to “model the process that produced these tokens.”

05 · LLM

Not a database: a learned function

f(context) → probabilities for the next token

Knowledge is distributed through weights and temporary activations rather than stored as ordinary rows in a database. During inference, your context flows through the trained network, a token is generated, appended to the context, and the process repeats.

Your prompt→Transformer→token↻

06 · Reasoning

Why prediction can become problem solving

Memorization can answer familiar examples. It cannot memorize every possible program, multiplication problem, or novel combination of ideas. Training therefore rewards reusable internal patterns and algorithms. Modern systems can also spend extra computation during inference before producing their final response.

07 · From model to system

The LLM is an engine, not the whole computer

A raw model does not automatically have durable memory, browse the web, operate servers, or send messages. Those capabilities come from the system built around it.

LLM+context+memory+tools+environment+feedback

This is the direction of 3DVR Operator: language as the primary interface to a larger computing system, while tools and services do the work underneath.
