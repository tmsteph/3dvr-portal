# Embodied sprites: the pet, and the inhabited world it is citizen one of

This note is the design deliverable for
[issue #3795, "World: embodied sprites"](https://github.com/tmsteph/3dvr-portal/issues/3795).
It is a companion to [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), which maps the living
functions from [#3625](https://github.com/tmsteph/3dvr-portal/issues/3625).
This note covers one of those functions, **have a body**, and the place
the body lives.

Origin, Thomas 2026-11-15: "I also want a 3d pet that would be cool,"
then, in the same breath, the real frame: "eventually it could turn into
a world where multiple agents can have bodies and inhabit, create,
whatever," and "Humans can join too and interact with the agents." The
portal definition he gave the same evening is the ground all of this
stands on: "The portal is basically supposed to be our OS, a collection
of apps and navigation to them. A portal into our world."

## The idea in one paragraph

A sprite is a mind: it thinks on its own every few hours, keeps a
journal, holds goals, lives on a budget, can fork. A journal is a hard
place to visit. So a sprite can be **embodied**: given a creature body
inside 3DVR World, the shared habitat the World shell already frames
(Preserve, Valley, Companion, Corner Office around one shared character
record). The sprite stays the brain. The body shows what the brain is
doing: where it roams, when it is pondering, what mood its last thought
left it in. The first embodied sprite is **Wander**, because roaming is
already its job. Its body is a small fox-like creature named **Pip**.

## Citizen one, not a one-off

The pet is designed as the first citizen of an inhabited world, not as
a standalone toy:

- **Sprite-plural bodies.** The body system takes a sprite snapshot
  (identity + state + journal digest) as its only input. Embodying
  Ember, Sprout, Flare or Scout later means producing their snapshots,
  not writing a second pet. Names, forms and temperaments differ per
  sprite; the plumbing does not.
- **A shared habitat.** The World is one place with many citizens in
  it, not one page per pet. Scenes (Valley first) are neighborhoods.
- **Humans are citizens too.** Human presence is not a new invention:
  portal accounts and Gun/SEA identity already say who a human is, and
  the World shell already shares one character record
  (`world.character.v1`) across its scenes, which is why the same
  person walks Preserve, Valley and Corner Office today. Agents join
  that same citizenry; they do not get a separate zoo.
- **Interaction is the direction.** In increasing depth: proximity (a
  citizen drifts toward a visitor), greeting, watching a sprite think
  (its ponder moments are its real ticks, made visible), and reading a
  sprite's journal at its home spot in the world. The journal is the
  soul file from
  [#3386](https://github.com/tmsteph/3dvr-portal/issues/3386); the body
  makes it visitable.
- **Creation is the far end.** Citizens eventually making things in
  the world, not just wandering it, is the stated direction. It is
  named here so nothing below paints it into a corner; it is not part
  of any current slice.

## How sprite state maps to body behavior

Every visible behavior is either driven by a real sprite state or is
plainly ambient animation. The mapping for v0:

| Sprite state | Body behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Identity: Wander, the free-curious explorer | Roams widely and slowly; no fixed home corner |
| Goals: follow odd questions, collect questions worth attention | Wander targets are scattered, not resource-driven; it does not gather or hoard |
| A tick happened (journal grew) | Ponder episodes: the body stops, sits, and a thought bubble pulses. Pondering is a tick made visible |
| Journal mood after the last tick (hand-read for the snapshot) | Ponder frequency and posture. Wander's tick 1 left it curious and holding an open question, so Pip ponders often, ears up |
| The held-open question in the journal | Carried in the snapshot verbatim; later slices can show it at the pet's home spot |
| Budget / spend | Not shown as a hunger meter. Energy is real but v0 does not gamify it |
| Paused / stopped sprite | Later slice: the body sleeps. (v0 snapshot is of an awake sprite) |

## What v0 is

- Pip in the existing Valley scene, drawn with canvas primitives in
  the scene's own style (the World's scenes are dependency-free 2D
  canvas today), wandering the ground line, sitting to ponder, with a
  name plate in the same style as the hero's.
- Behavior driven by a **static snapshot** embedded in the page,
  generated by hand from Wander's real runtime state on OVH
  (`/opt/3dvr-sprites/wander/`: identity, state, journal), dated and
  sourced in the snapshot itself.
- A test pinning the snapshot fields and the pet's update/draw
  wiring, in the style of the existing Valley tests.

## What v0 deliberately is NOT

- **Not live.** The page does not read the sprite. Wander will think
  again tonight and Pip will not know until the snapshot is refreshed.
  Live wiring (a small served endpoint the World can read) is the next
  slice, and until it lands, nobody should describe Pip as live.
- **Not a pet game.** No feeding, no hunger, no coins, no shop, no
  streaks. A sprite eats inference, not clicks; inventing a food
  economy would be a fake layer over a real one.
- **Not a chat window.** You cannot talk to Pip in v0. Interaction is
  a later slice, and it will route to the sprite's real mind when it
  comes, not to a script.
- **Not yet 3D.** Thomas said "3d pet"; v0's body renders in
  Valley's 2D canvas because that is the World that exists. The
  inhabited world is the point; a WebGL body in a 3D scene is a later
  slice when a scene warrants it.
- **Not multi-citizen yet.** The architecture is plural; the v0
  population is one fox.

## Safety and honesty invariants

Inherited from DESIGN.md and unchanged here: the owner stop always
wins (a stopped sprite's body must stop too, when live wiring
lands); a body never grants the sprite new powers (embodiment is
display and presence, not a new action surface); and no behavior may
be shown that claims a sprite state which does not exist. If a
snapshot is stale, the honest thing is to show its date, not to fake
freshness.

## Later slices

1. Refresh path: a tiny generator that rebuilds the snapshot from the
   runtime home, run after ticks, so the body is hours-fresh instead
   of hand-fresh.
2. Live wiring: the World reads sprite state from a served endpoint;
   ticks become visible near-real-time; a stopped sprite sleeps.
3. More citizens: embody the other sprites, each with its own form.
4. Human-agent interaction: proximity, greeting, journal-at-home.
5. Presence in the /os/ launcher
   ([#3792](https://github.com/tmsteph/3dvr-portal/pull/3792)).
6. A WebGL body in a true 3D scene.
7. Creation: citizens building in the world.
