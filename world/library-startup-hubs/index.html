Portal World

Civic design experiment

Libraries should be
startup hubs.

Libraries already provide trusted public space, internet, power, knowledge, and community access. With a few intentional additions, they can become neighborhood infrastructure for remote work, experimentation, entrepreneurship, and local collaboration.

North star: make it possible for anyone to turn knowledge into a project, a project into a livelihood, and useful work into stronger local communities.

The opportunity

Public knowledge infrastructure can become public creation infrastructure.

A person should be able to walk into a library with an idea and find the basic conditions needed to work on it.

That means dependable internet, power, comfortable workspace, research access, places to talk, places to focus, and pathways to meet collaborators or get practical help. Libraries already have most of this foundation.

Coworking layer

Make ordinary modern work feel normal in the library.

Conversation zones

Clearly marked areas where normal indoor speaking and video calls with headphones are welcome.

Quiet zones

Preserve genuinely quiet areas for reading, study, and focused work.

Drop-in desks

Power, strong Wi-Fi, comfortable seating, and workspace without requiring a purchase or membership.

Privacy booths

Small spaces for interviews, telehealth, confidential calls, and focused meetings.

Meeting rooms

Keep reservable rooms for teams, workshops, longer sessions, and group collaboration.

Longer stays welcome

Design some seating around people doing several hours of real work, not only short visits.

Startup layer

Help people move from learning to building.

Mentor hours

Invite local builders, accountants, designers, technologists, and business owners to offer office hours.

Maker access

Connect software, fabrication, media, electronics, and other maker resources where practical.

Business help

Make local licensing, incorporation, financing, procurement, and small-business resources easy to find.

Demo nights

Give neighbors a recurring place to show what they are building and find users, collaborators, or customers.

Project boards

Surface local needs, open projects, volunteer opportunities, and people looking for collaborators.

Open knowledge

Encourage reusable documentation, open-source tools, and lessons that the next builder can start from.

Smallest useful experiment

Start with one branch and one clearly marked coworking zone.

Normal voices allowed. Headphones required. Quiet areas stay quiet. Add a weekly builder or mentor session and measure what people actually use.

The goal is not to turn every library into a loud office. It is to let different modes coexist intentionally, then build outward from evidence.

Community flywheel

Knowledge → project → business → community → shared knowledge.

Libraries can help people consume knowledge and create with it.

When a local project succeeds, its founders can return as mentors, share tools, sponsor events, contribute documentation, hire locally, or help the next person start. That turns the library into a lightweight neighborhood innovation network rather than a one-way service.

Local starting point

Prototype it where people are already trying to work.

This idea grew out of using libraries around San Diego as dependable places for internet and air conditioning while needing to take ordinary calls and do real work. The first version does not need a major construction project: designate space, clarify norms, invite local builders, and learn from usage.
