Pattern 001

Everyday drawstring pants

Start with the easiest useful garment: relaxed pants with a linen drawstring and a simple casing. The first version deliberately uses a pair of loose pants you already like as the pattern.

01

Choose the linen

Use medium-weight 100% linen, roughly 5–7 oz/yd², for a first everyday pair.

Before cutting: wash and dry the fabric the way you plan to care for the finished pants.

02

Trace a pair that fits

Fold a loose pair of pants in half. Trace the front and back leg shapes onto paper or directly onto folded linen.

Add about 5/8 in (1.5 cm) around sewn edges and extra height at the waist for the drawstring casing.

03

Sew the legs

Sew each front to its matching back along the outer and inner leg seams. Finish raw edges with a flat-felled, French, or turned-and-stitched seam.

04

Join the rise

Turn one leg right-side out, place it inside the other leg, match the crotch curve, and sew the full rise as one continuous seam.

05

Build the linen casing

Fold the waist edge inward twice to create a casing wide enough for the drawstring. Leave a small opening at the front.

For an all-fiber version, hand-stitch the opening like a buttonhole instead of adding a metal eyelet.

06

Make the drawstring

Cut a long linen strip, fold the raw edges inward, fold again, and stitch it into a narrow cord. Feed it through the casing and knot the ends.

Prototype rule: Make the first pair roomy. Wear them, wash them, mark what you would change, then update the pattern. The pattern grows from real bodies instead of forcing bodies into fixed sizing.

The system

Less fashion. More human infrastructure.

Natural first

Prefer linen and other simple natural materials over complex blends and hidden synthetics.

Repair forever

Construction should be understandable enough that the owner can patch, resew, resize, or reuse the cloth.

Open patterns

Patterns are instructions, not secrets. Copy them, adapt them, teach them, and improve them.

Local skill

A garment can be made at home, by a neighborhood tailor, or by a small local sewing circle.

Few good pieces

Build a small wardrobe where the same pieces work at home, at work, outdoors, and while traveling.

Designed to evolve

Every repair and modification can become knowledge for the next version of the pattern.
