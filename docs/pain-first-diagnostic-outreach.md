# Pain-First Diagnostic Outreach

## Decision

Make pain-first diagnostic outreach the default cold-sales motion for 3DVR when there is a verifiable public problem.

The sequence is:

**visible pain -> precise note -> small paid diagnostic -> excellent delivery -> larger fix or ongoing support**

Do not lead with a broad list of capabilities such as websites, automation, AI, CRM, or consulting. Start with one concrete problem the prospect can recognize.

## Why this exists

Generic outreach asks a stranger to imagine a future need. Pain-first outreach starts with a problem that already exists and gives the buyer a small, low-risk next step.

The first sale should usually be narrow enough that the buyer can say yes without a long sales process. The diagnostic earns trust and gives both sides evidence about whether a larger engagement makes sense.

## Qualified lead

A lead qualifies only when all of the following are true:

1. There is a public, verifiable signal of a real problem.
2. The problem is plausibly costing time, leads, reliability, or customer trust.
3. 3DVR can inspect or improve it without needing privileged access before authorization.
4. The first paid step can be tightly scoped and reversible.
5. The outreach can describe the issue without hype, fear, or invented details.

Good examples include a broken customer path, dead booking or contact link, confusing mobile flow, missing or contradictory calls to action, obvious workflow friction, or another observable operational problem.

For security-sensitive findings, do not send exploit details or alarming language. Describe the issue at a high level, minimize disclosure, and wait for explicit authorization before any invasive verification.

## Offer ladder

Default first offer: a **$99 diagnostic**.

Reasonable range: **$50-$200** when the scope clearly justifies it.

The diagnostic should produce a useful artifact even if no second project follows, such as:

- a short findings summary,
- before/after evidence where safe,
- a prioritized fix list,
- a tiny remediation if explicitly included,
- a recommended next step with a separate price.

Do not disguise a sales call as a diagnostic. The buyer should receive something useful for the money.

## Outreach message

Keep the first message short. Use only facts supported by the public signal.

Structure:

1. Name the public signal.
2. Explain the practical consequence in plain language.
3. Offer one small paid diagnostic.
4. Ask one easy yes/no question.

Example:

> Hi [Business] team,
>
> I noticed [specific public problem]. That can make it harder for customers to [practical consequence].
>
> I can check the path end to end and send you a short findings + fix plan for $99. No rebuild required.
>
> Want me to take a look?
>
> Thomas

Commercial outreach must still pass the existing 3DVR disclosure, postal-address, opt-out, and send-window checks.

## CRM record

For every pain-first lead, capture these fields before sending:

- `primaryPain`: the concrete problem.
- `lastSignal`: the public evidence and where it was observed.
- `painSeverity`: Low, Medium, High, or Critical.
- `urgency`: low, medium, or high.
- `offerAmount`: diagnostic price.
- `nextBestAction`: usually `Offer paid diagnostic` until the lead replies.
- `source`: campaign or discovery source.

If there is no trustworthy public signal, do not pretend there is one. Either research further or skip the lead.

## First experiment

Run a batch of **10 fresh leads** before scaling.

- 5 owner-led service businesses with a visible customer-flow problem.
- 3 professional-services firms with a visible intake or follow-up problem.
- 2 small agencies or technical operators with an obvious operational bottleneck.

Do not message existing personal or professional contacts under this cold-outreach rule. Known contacts remain approval-gated.

Send during normal recipient business hours. Keep volume low enough to review every message individually.

## Success rule

After 10 qualified touches:

- **2+ real replies or 1 paid diagnostic:** continue and tighten the segment.
- **Replies but no paid step:** improve the diagnostic promise, evidence, or price.
- **0 meaningful replies:** stop. Do not increase volume. Revisit the problem quality and message.
- **Any complaint, factual error, or deliverability warning:** pause and investigate immediately.

The goal is not email volume. The goal is repeatable conversion from a real observable problem into a small paid engagement.

## Expansion rule

A diagnostic does not automatically justify a larger project. After delivery, propose the smallest next step supported by the findings.

Typical expansion paths:

- one-time remediation,
- website or intake-flow cleanup,
- workflow automation,
- monitoring and maintenance,
- Builder or Embedded support when ongoing work is genuinely useful.

Keep the diagnostic and the follow-on scope separate so the buyer can stop after receiving the first paid result.
