# Distributed Ownership: The 3DVR Manifesto

> The future of automation should be distributed ownership, not just concentrated productivity.

## Why
AI can reduce the cost of producing software and services, but higher productivity does not guarantee higher wages, lower prices, or broadly shared prosperity. The outcome depends on competition, bargaining power, access, and who owns the productive assets. Automation can displace work and can also enable new markets. We should test which effects actually occur rather than assume either outcome.

## Our promise
Help people **learn, build, own, earn, and share**.

1. **Learn:** Teach the underlying concepts; keep a human-first learning path.
2. **Build:** Make open tools, prototypes, and real-world validation accessible.
3. **Own:** Prefer portable data, open formats, self-hosting, and user-controlled credentials and assets.
4. **Earn:** Help builders find real customer needs, sell useful solutions, and build recurring revenue without promising effortless income.
5. **Share:** Publish reusable knowledge and open-source improvements so others can participate.

## Operator product principles
- **Learn mode:** The human writes; AI explains, quizzes, and reviews on request.
- **Build Together mode:** Human and AI co-design and implement, with visible decisions and tests.
- **Autopilot mode:** AI can draft, implement, and test within granted permissions; consequential external actions require human approval.
- Users can switch modes per task. No mode should lock away code, data, or export.
- Preserve opportunities for debugging, experimentation, and learning rather than automating every interesting challenge.
- Make costs, ownership, deployment, provenance, and rollback legible.

## Economic experiments
- Track whether AI saves time *and* improves user skills and satisfaction.
- Measure the share of projects that users can export and run independently.
- Test whether small builders can win paying customers, retain ownership, and generate sustainable net revenue.
- Monitor concentration risks: provider lock-in, infrastructure costs, distribution gatekeepers, and platform dependency.
- Treat productivity, employment, prices, and wages as empirical questions, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Implementation roadmap
1. Publish this manifesto in the Portal's philosophy/learning area with a clear link from the worker-to-owner journey.
2. Prototype a per-task Learn / Build Together / Autopilot selector in Operator.
3. Add export/self-host checks and permission disclosures to the task completion flow.
4. Run a small paid customer experiment and publish anonymized results.
5. Review feedback and revise requirements using the skunkworks approach.

**North star:** More people should be able to understand, control, and benefit from the systems they help create.
