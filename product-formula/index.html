3DVR product principle

Make the familiar feel magical.

The goal is not to build the most technically impressive thing. Build something people understand immediately, enjoy returning to, and naturally want to show someone else.

1

Start with familiar behavior.

Anchor the product in something people already understand: chatting, looking through a window, docking a phone, sharing work, checking a schedule.

2

Wrap it in something memorable.

Add the part that feels distinctly 3DVR: spatial depth, ambient intelligence, unusual physical interaction, delightful transitions, or a strong sense of presence.

3

Create a useful ritual.

Give people a reason to come back because the product genuinely helps them, not because it nags them. Daily usefulness beats manufactured engagement.

4

Give it emotional meaning.

The experience should support agency, creativity, calm, connection, mastery, freedom, or wonder. Technology should feel human.

5

Make sharing natural.

Replace social pressure with community. Create moments, artifacts, progress, and ideas people are genuinely excited to share.

6

Offer a truly better premium layer.

Paid upgrades should add real capability, convenience, compute, hardware, service, or professional value. Never cripple the core experience just to force conversion.

The instant product test

Can someone feel the point in seconds?

Do I understand what this is immediately?
Does it feel meaningfully different?
Would I want to use it again tomorrow?
Would I want to show it to somebody?
Does the paid layer make the product better instead of merely less restricted?

Trust first

No pressure loops. Build things worth returning to.

3DVR should grow through usefulness, curiosity, open-source participation, human relationships, and real value. Automation can amplify authentic relationships, but it should not manufacture urgency or dependency.
