Buoyancy–Gravity Elevator

Open-source experiment: a chain of capsules rises through water and descends through air. Can pressure recovery improve useful energy storage?

Experiment plan

  1. Build a clear 2 m water column and eight-capsule elevator chain.
  2. Measure capsule displacement, mass and lift with a force gauge.
  3. Measure work to introduce a capsule at the bottom under pressure.
  4. Test a pressure-balanced transfer chamber and measure recovered energy.
  5. Compare mechanical output to all input work over a complete cycle.

Use low-pressure, non-fragile parts; secure the column against tipping. Do not seal or pressurize improvised containers.

Physics

Buoyancy work ≈ ρgVh. At constant volume, bottom insertion against hydrostatic pressure also costs ≈ ρgVh. A closed cycle cannot produce net energy without an external source.

Potential applications: energy storage, water-level energy harvesting, and educational demonstrations.
