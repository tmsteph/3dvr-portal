A modular kit, designed in the open

One kit for living anywhere.

Not separate products. One modular way of living that moves with you: a backpack, a tent, a desk, an e-bike, a car, and a yurt that all fit together.

Everything earns its place. Home is something you carry and set up, not somewhere you are stuck. This page is the journal of the kit as it gets designed, one honest piece at a time.

How it is built

The pattern.

The same rules for every module, whatever it turns out to be.

Hotel-grade toughness

If it survives banquet staff and back-of-house corners, it survives travel. Weak and clever loses to strong and simple.

Natural materials first

Linen before synthetics. Wood, metal, canvas: materials a person can understand, repair, and replace close to home.

Small footprints

Every module packs down, tucks away, or folds flat when it is not working. Nothing gets to be bulky for no reason.

Multi-use parts

A frame that is also furniture. A tray that is also a mount. If a part only does one job, it has to be a very good job.

Finished designs get the full recipe published: design, materials, cost, instructions, repairs. That is the Open Object Standard the Everyday Life projects already follow.

Roots

Standing on open shoulders.

This idea has a family tree, and it is a public one.

Open Source Ecology designs the machines and systems a small group needs to live well, and publishes the plans so anyone can build and improve them. Their Global Village Construction Set is that idea at village scale. The System is the same idea at family scale: open designs, shared freely, forked by anyone.

It grows out of Open Source Everyday Life, the portal's open-recipe program for everyday things, where the backpack and the linen work already live. The wider thinking is in the open source field guide, and the long road, cars, tents, and yurts included, is sketched on the expansion path.

Where it is going

The farm is where the yurt stops moving.

The kit is the bridge. The land is the destination.

Thomas and Kristin are saving toward a food-forest, off-grid farm. The System is how to live well until then: mobile, light, and whole. When the land is real, the yurt becomes the first building, the desk stands in it, and the kit becomes the seed of the farm instead of a way to travel.

Until then it is references, sketches, and prototypes, kept in the open. Earth is a garden planet →
