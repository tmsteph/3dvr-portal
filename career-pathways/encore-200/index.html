Five-minute mental model

1. Discover → 2. Clarify → 3. Own → 4. Execute → 5. Recover → 6. Close the loop.

Most of Level 200 is this loop applied to customers, coworkers, pressure, details, and professional performance.

On show site: ask what “success” means, repeat the requirement back, name the owner, set the next update time, fix friction fast, then confirm the client is actually good.

Professional Skills 200

Customer discovery
needslisteningpriorities

Do not start with the solution. Ask open questions, listen for constraints, confirm what matters most, and repeat the requirement back in plain language.

AV translation: “Is the priority room coverage, broadcast quality, recording, or keeping the stage clean?” That one question can prevent an hour of wrong setup.
Resilience under pressure

Separate what happened from what you can control next. Use the smallest useful next action, communicate early, and avoid turning a setback into a spiral.

AV translation: bad cable / late deck / missing input: stabilize the show first, explain the workaround second, root-cause it after the room is safe.
Working well with others

Clarify roles, make handoffs explicit, disagree about the task rather than the person, and surface uncertainty before it becomes a show problem.

AV translation: “I own playback. You own confidence. If either changes, call it on comms.”
Service mindset

Ownership beats deflection. Show empathy, make the next step obvious, update before the customer has to ask, and finish the loop instead of assuming the issue disappeared.

AV translation: if the client asks the wrong department, you still own getting them to the right answer.
Self-confidence + self-coaching

Confidence is easier when it is evidence-based: preparation, past wins, and a clear next action. After a show, review: what happened, what worked, what changes next time?

AV translation: replace “I think this should work” with “I verified signal at these three points; the remaining risk is here.”
Memory + business communication

Externalize memory. Chunk information, write critical details down, use cues, repeat important handoffs, and match the communication channel to urgency.

AV translation: comms for immediate action; chat/email for durable details; show notes for anything the next shift must inherit.
Personal accountability + professional performance

Own commitments, deadlines, quality and escalation. If a promise is at risk, say so early with a recovery plan. Professionalism is consistency under imperfect conditions.

Service Level 200

Attention to detail

Use deliberate checks for high-impact details instead of trusting familiarity. Checklists are not weakness; they protect attention when the environment gets noisy.

AV translation: source → route → processor → destination → confidence. Verify the whole signal path, not just the device you touched.
Interpreting customer priorities

Customers may care about speed, accuracy, appearance, flexibility, reassurance, or cost differently. Listen for which dimension matters most before optimizing.

Engaging customers

Be present, approachable, proactive and concise. Set expectations in advance and update when reality changes.

AV translation: “We have five minutes of testing left; I’ll give you a go/no-go at 8:42.”
Effortless experience

Reduce repeated questions, unnecessary handoffs and invisible waiting. Anticipate the next step and make the path obvious.

AV translation: the client should not need to learn Encore’s org chart to get a microphone fixed.
Service acumen

Understand enough of the operation to connect a customer request to the people, equipment, timing and tradeoffs needed to deliver it.

AV translation: translate “make it more exciting” into lighting looks, walk-in music, IMAG choices, timing and budget.
Service recovery

Listen without arguing, acknowledge the impact, clarify the desired recovery, act quickly, keep the customer updated, then verify the fix.

AV translation: recovery is not complete when signal returns; it is complete when the client knows what happened next and trusts the room again.
Brand service elements

Consistency matters: people should experience the same basic ownership, clarity and professionalism regardless of which technician or property they encounter.

Fast self-check

Before a show issue: What does the customer actually care about most?

During it: What can I own right now, and when is my next update?

After it: Did I verify the fix with the customer and capture what we should change next time?
