Architecture

Separate the laptop from the compute generation.

Balthazar's strongest idea was the separation between the useful physical laptop and the replaceable compute module. We want to preserve that philosophy while adapting it to hardware we can obtain and test today.

01 / Compute

Replaceable compute module

CPU, RAM, and board-level compute live in a replaceable module with a documented interface to the rest of the laptop.

02 / I/O

Stable laptop interfaces

Display, keyboard, trackpad, storage, USB, networking, audio, and power should remain useful across several compute generations.

03 / Service

Designed to come apart

Favor screws, documented fasteners, standard tools, replaceable cells and modules, and parts that can be made or sourced independently.

Prototype targets

Use existing boards before inventing new silicon.

Our first job is to prove the laptop architecture. The compute module can improve as RISC-V and open hardware improve.

Bridge platform

x86 compute modules

Documented x86 modules can help validate power, display, keyboard, battery, thermals, and enclosure design while RISC-V catches up.

Design target

A computer you can keep.

These are goals, not final specifications. The project stays intentionally flexible until real prototypes teach us what works.

  • ComputeReplaceable module; RISC-V first where practical, other open/documented options welcome.
  • StorageStandard replaceable NVMe or similarly serviceable storage.
  • MemoryReplaceable where the module architecture allows it.
  • DisplayCommon, documented interface with a replaceable panel assembly.
  • InputReplaceable keyboard and trackpad modules.
  • BatteryServiceable pack with published electrical and mechanical information.
  • FirmwareMinimize opaque firmware and document every unavoidable closed dependency.
  • OSLinux-first with upstream support preferred over vendor-only software stacks.

Build in public
